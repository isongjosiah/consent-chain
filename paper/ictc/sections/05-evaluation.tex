\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate \sysname on unlearning effectiveness, on-chain cost
(PureChain versus Polygon Amoy), scalability of round validation to
$N\!=\!500$, and utility recovery, plus two on-chain demos validating
the liveness and verifiability properties; the full reproducibility
recipe is in supplementary \texttt{docs/eval-protocol.md}.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

The primary federation is a 9-client N-BaIoT autoencoder anomaly
detector~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot} (one client per device), a symmetric
MLP ($115\!\to\!75\!\to\!50\!\to\!25\!\to\!10$) trained with FedAvg over
30 rounds (Flower, seed 42). At round 30 we
simulate a GDPR Art.~17 erasure of client 5, triggering one of the
three approximate methods or exact 8-client retraining. On-chain experiments run against live
contracts on gas-free PureChain (PoA$^2$) and gas-based Polygon Amoy
(PoS), with $\mathit{VC}$ five ECDSA keys, $t=3$, and $\dmax=1000$
blocks.

Three observations stand out. All three approximate methods produce
UAs the chain accepts (shadow $0.438$/$0.254$/$0.523$ for gradient
ascent/SISA-Fed/FedEraser; LiRA and label-only well below $\tauMIA$).
The hero result: the naive-retrain baseline---never trained on client
5---yields shadow $0.584>\tauMIA$ and is \emph{rejected}, because shadow
reads client 5's distinctive traffic distribution, not
membership~\cite{shokri2017mia}, a confound LiRA ($0.045$) and
label-only ($0.014$) avoid. Even the no-unlearning baseline sits near random ($0.55$/$0.05$/$0.01$):
this federation memorises client 5 only weakly, so the gate certifies
the post-model leaks no more than chance---its work is rejecting the
confound, not proving prior memorisation; Section~\ref{sec:eval-casas}
supplies the complementary high-memorisation regime in which the gate
must, and does, reject. All three approximate UAs finalise end-to-end
on PureChain to \textsc{Unlearned}.

Per-data-subject lifecycle cost is $\$0$ on PureChain and $\$0.00120$
on Amoy. Gas counts differ modestly between the two chains, but the
dominant factor at scale is gas \emph{price} (zero versus
$\sim\!30$ gwei), not gas count.

$\texttt{validateRound}(N)$ scales exactly linearly in the participant
count. PureChain runs the full $N\!\in\![5,500]$ sweep live
($R^2\!\approx\!1$); Amoy, measured at $N\!\in\!\{5,10\}$ and
extrapolated linearly, projects to $\$0.0176$ per $N\!=\!500$ round.
At a typical 100-round/day cadence that is $\$1.76$/day per deployment
on Amoy versus $\$0$ on PureChain: gas-free infrastructure is a
different cost class, not an optimisation. Two short PureChain runs also
confirm the properties empirically---a $\dmax\!=\!2$ timeout fires
\texttt{ComplianceViolation} (liveness), and a $t$-of-5 sweep exercises
the acceptance predicate's two branches (verifiability).

\subsection{Utility recovery after revocation}
\label{sec:eval-utility}

Resuming FL for ten rounds after dropping client 5, the post-revoke
model reaches reconstruction error $0.4720$, $6.1\%$ \emph{better} than
the 9-client baseline ($0.5025$)---client 5 was an outlier whose
removal tightens the fit. Gradient ascent ($0.4726$) and FedEraser
($0.4698$) match this at no utility cost; SISA-Fed pays $+29\%$
($0.6081$ by round 40), consistent with its strongest forgetting.

\subsection{Cross-domain validation: the high-memorisation regime}
\label{sec:eval-casas}

N-BaIoT and CASAS span two memorisation regimes. N-BaIoT's baselines
all sit near random, so the gate correctly \emph{admits}; the 5-client
CASAS BiLSTM is the opposite---pre-unlearning shadow MIA reaches
$0.979$ and the gate must \emph{reject}. It does: gradient ascent
($0.980$), FedEraser ($0.840$), and even exact naive retrain ($0.846$)
stay above $\tauMIA$ and are refused, while only SISA-Fed
($0.499$/$0.035$/$0.000$) clears all three attacks. The pair thus
exercises both branches of the acceptance predicate on identical
contracts; full results appear in the journal version.
