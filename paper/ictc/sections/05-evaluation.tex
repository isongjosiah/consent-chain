\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate \sysname on unlearning effectiveness, on-chain cost
(PureChain versus Polygon Amoy), scalability of round validation to
$N\!=\!500$, and utility recovery, plus two on-chain demos validating
the liveness and verifiability properties; the full reproducibility
recipe is in supplementary \texttt{docs/eval-protocol.md}.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

The primary federation is a 9-client N-BaIoT anomaly
detector~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot}, one autoencoder per device, a
symmetric MLP $115\!\to\!75\!\to\!50\!\to\!25\!\to\!10$ trained with
FedAvg for 30 rounds (Flower, seed 42). A GDPR Art.~17 erasure of client
5 at round 30 triggers one of the three approximate methods or exact
8-client retraining. Contracts run live on gas-free PureChain (PoA$^2$)
and gas-based Amoy (PoS), with $\mathit{VC}$ of five ECDSA keys, $t=3$,
$\dmax=1000$ blocks.

The gate accepts all three approximate methods: shadow MIA reads
$0.438$/$0.254$/$0.523$ (gradient ascent/SISA-Fed/FedEraser), with LiRA
and label-only far below $\tauMIA$. Strikingly, it \emph{rejects} exact
naive retraining, which never trained on client 5: shadow scores
$0.584>\tauMIA$ because it reads client 5's distinctive traffic
distribution, not membership~\cite{shokri2017mia}, a confound LiRA
($0.045$) and label-only ($0.014$) sidestep. The no-unlearning baseline
already sits near random ($0.55$/$0.05$/$0.01$), so this federation
memorises client 5 only weakly: here the gate's role is rejecting that
distributional confound, not detecting strong leakage.
Section~\ref{sec:eval-casas} supplies the high-memorisation regime where
it must, and does, reject real residual signal. All three accepted UAs
finalise end-to-end on PureChain to \textsc{Unlearned}.

Per-subject lifecycle cost is $0$ on PureChain and $0.00120$ on Amoy; the gap is gas price (zero versus ~ 30 gwei), not count. validateRound(N) scales exactly linearly: PureChain runs the full $N \in [5, 500]$ sweep live ($R^2 \approx 1$), while Amoy, measured at $N \in \{5, 10\}$ and extrapolated, projects to $0.0176$ per $N=500$ round, i.e. $1.76$/day at 100 rounds/day versus 0. Alongside the gas footprint, off-chain evaluation overhead remains low on the orchestrator, with validateRound adding a negligible computational latency of $\approx 3.2\text{ ms}$ per FL round at $N=500$. Gas-free infrastructure is thus a different cost class, not an optimisation. Two short PureChain runs confirm the remaining properties: a $\dmax\!=\!2$ timeout fires \texttt{ComplianceViolation}
(liveness), and a $t$-of-5 sweep exercises both branches of the
acceptance predicate (verifiability).

\subsection{Utility recovery after revocation}
\label{sec:eval-utility}

Resuming FL for ten rounds after dropping client 5, the post-revoke
model reaches reconstruction error $0.4720$, $6.1\%$ \emph{better} than
the 9-client baseline ($0.5025$), since client 5 was an outlier.
Gradient ascent ($0.4726$) and FedEraser ($0.4698$) match this at no
utility cost, while SISA-Fed pays $+29\%$ ($0.6081$ by round 40),
consistent with its stronger forgetting (Figure~\ref{fig:f2}).

\subsection{Cross-domain validation: the high-memorisation regime}
\label{sec:eval-casas}

CASAS provides the complementary high-memorisation regime: where
N-BaIoT's baselines sit near random, the 5-client CASAS BiLSTM reaches
pre-unlearning shadow MIA $0.979$, so the gate must \emph{reject}, and
does: gradient ascent ($0.980$), FedEraser ($0.840$), and even exact
naive retrain ($0.846$) stay above $\tauMIA$ and are refused, while only
SISA-Fed ($0.499$/$0.035$/$0.000$) clears all three attacks. The two
domains thus exercise both branches of the acceptance predicate on
identical contracts; full results appear in the journal version.
