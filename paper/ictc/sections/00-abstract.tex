Federated learning over consumer IoT devices must now honour
data-subject withdrawal under GDPR, the EU AI Act, and CCPA: consent
can be revoked and the trained model must demonstrably forget. Prior
work treats consent management, machine unlearning, and on-chain
auditability as disjoint threads. We close the
\emph{consent--unlearning--verification triangle} with \sysname, a
protocol binding all three in a single chain-enforced pipeline: a
Consent Lifecycle State Machine (\clsm) as soulbound ERC-721 tokens; a
Verifiable Unlearning Protocol gating on-chain acceptance on three
independent membership-inference attacks; a three-contract Solidity
architecture on a gas-free (PureChain, PoA$^2$) and a gas-based
(Polygon Amoy, PoS) chain; and a dual-domain evaluation on N-BaIoT and
CASAS. Per-subject lifecycle cost is \$0 on PureChain versus \$0.00120
on Amoy; the gate accepts approximate unlearning
($\text{MIA}\!<\!0.55$) yet rejects naive retraining---whose
distributional uniqueness inflates MIA---and any attestation lacking a
3-of-5 verifier threshold, proving it faithful to MIA evidence rather
than the controller's claim.
