\section{System Model and Threat Model}
\label{sec:system-model}

\subsection{Entities}

\sysname has six entities. The data subject $\mathit{DS}_i$ controls
the private key of one soulbound Consent NFT; the data controller
$\mathit{DC}$ orchestrates the FL round loop and triggers unlearning on
revocation; each participant device $\mathit{FL}_k$ runs local SGD; the
Verifier Committee $\mathit{VC}=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}$ holds $n=5$
registered ECDSA keys with default threshold $t=3$; and three on-chain
contracts---\texttt{ConsentRegistry} (CR), \texttt{UnlearningOracle}
(UO), and \texttt{FLGatekeeper} (GK)---implement the protocol; any
chain reader is an \emph{auditor}.

\subsection{Federated learning baseline}

We adopt FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}, aggregating validated clients
as $\theta^{(r+1)}=\sum_{k\in K_r}(n_k/\!\sum_{j\in K_r}n_j)\,
\theta^{(r+1)}_k$, where $K_r$ is the participant set returned by
$\mathit{GK}.\mathtt{validateRound}$ and $n_k$ the local set size. The
novelty: $K_r$ must be that return value, not an orchestrator-chosen
subset---consent enforced at the membership level, not the gradient
level.

\subsection{Consent Lifecycle State Machine}
\label{sec:clsm-formal}

The \clsm is a deterministic finite automaton
$\clsm=(S,\Sigma,\delta,s_0,F)$ over six states $S$
(\textsc{Inactive}, \textsc{Active}, \textsc{Suspended},
\textsc{Revoked}, \textsc{Expired}, \textsc{Unlearned}; start state
\textsc{Inactive}, absorbing terminal $F=\{\textsc{Unlearned}\}$) and
eight events $\Sigma$ (\texttt{grant}, \texttt{modify},
\texttt{suspend}, \texttt{resume}, \texttt{revoke}, \texttt{tick},
\texttt{expire}, \texttt{unlearn\_verified}). Its nine $\delta$
transitions are: \texttt{grant} activates a token; \texttt{modify} and
\texttt{tick} are \textsc{Active} self-edges;
\texttt{suspend}/\texttt{resume} toggle \textsc{Suspended};
\texttt{revoke} and \texttt{expire} leave \textsc{Active} for
\textsc{Revoked}/\textsc{Expired}; and \texttt{unlearn\_verified}
(oracle-only) moves either to the absorbing \textsc{Unlearned}.

\texttt{ConsentRegistry} instantiates \clsm exactly: every $\delta$
edge is one external function, and the soulbound constraint (no
\texttt{transferFrom}) ensures only $\mathit{DS}_i$ may transition
token $i$.

\subsection{Threat model}

We defend four in-scope adversaries: a \emph{lazy orchestrator} (GK
reverts on pending unlearning and emits \texttt{ComplianceViolation}
after the $\dmax$ timeout), a \emph{deceptive controller} (three MIAs
plus VC $t$-of-$n$ signatures), a \emph{consent forger} (soulbound NFT
and \texttt{dsOwner} check), and a \emph{replay attacker} (UA binding
$(\text{chainid},\text{tokenId},\text{hashPre})$). The committee is the
trust floor: $\ge t$ colluding verifiers can certify an invalid
attestation, so verifier-incentive design is future work. Colluding
participants (an orthogonal DP layer) and six further classes are out
of scope (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}); the full per-adversary model
is in supplementary \texttt{docs/threat-model.md}.

\subsection{Formal properties}
\label{sec:properties}

\sysname must satisfy four properties. \textbf{Consent integrity
(safety):} every participant \texttt{validateRound} admits is
\textsc{Active} with the round purpose in its purpose set, and no
$\delta$-path returns \textsc{Revoked} to \textsc{Active}.
\textbf{Enforceability (liveness):} within $\dmax$ blocks of a
revocation, either a valid UA drives the token to \textsc{Unlearned} or
the gatekeeper logs a public \texttt{ComplianceViolation}---degrading to
``no training,'' not ``unconsented training.'' \textbf{Verifiability:}
the oracle accepts a UA iff all three MIA scores fall below $\tauMIA$
and $t$-of-$n$ verifiers sign it. \textbf{Audit completeness:} every
transition, attestation, violation, and model-hash update emits an
indexed event. Safety and audit hold by construction; liveness and
verifiability are shown empirically in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation},
with mechanised proofs left to future work.
