\section{introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\vspace{-0.2cm}
Federated learning (FL) trains a shared model without centralising
client data~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}, often the only legally viable
option in consumer IoT~\cite{isong2026xs}. But GDPR Articles~7 and~17 demand
purpose-granular, revocable consent whose withdrawal propagates into the
model. Federated unlearning (FUL) erases a client's data and influence,
yet a bare withdrawal is a one-shot trigger, not a scoped state binding
round selection, so training on revoked data may continue unrecorded;
and approximate unlearning, the only kind tractable at FL scale, offers
no guarantee, fails \emph{silently}, and is self-reported by the
controller that owes it. \sysname couples \emph{dynamic consent} with
\emph{verifiable machine unlearning} in one chain-enforced protocol,
closing both gaps.

\sysname makes four contributions: (1)~a Consent Lifecycle State Machine
(\clsm), a soulbound-ERC-721 automaton of six states and nine
transitions whose safety and liveness hold by construction; (2)~a Verifiable Unlearning
Protocol (\vup), method-agnostic, accepting a UA iff three
\emph{independent} MIAs (shadow~\cite{shokri2017mia},
LiRA~\cite{carlini2022lira}, label-only~\cite{choquettechoo2021labelonly})
fall below $\tauMIA$ under $t$-of-$n$ ECDSA signatures; (3)~three
Solidity contracts (ConsentRegistry, UnlearningOracle, FLGatekeeper) on
both a gas-free (PureChain, PoA$^2$) and gas-based (Amoy, PoS) chain, the
first side-by-side cost comparison; and (4)~a dual-domain evaluation
(9-client N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot} autoencoder, 5-client CASAS
BiLSTM) over three approximate-unlearning methods plus exact retraining,
measuring forgetting, cost, and utility recovery.
