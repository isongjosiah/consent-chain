\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Federated learning (FL) trains a shared model across decentralised
clients without centralising their data~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}, and is often
the only legally viable option in consumer IoT. Beyond technical
privacy, a second, equally binding axis is the right to be forgotten: consent
must stay purpose-granular and revocable, and revocation should propagate into
the trained model itself; these obligations are grounded in GDPR Articles~7 and~17.

While Federated unlearning (FUL) has emerged as a technique to erase the data
and any influence of the data on the FL model, FUL presumes a withdrawal signal, 
but that signal is a one-shot trigger fired after the fact, not an enforced state: it cannot express
purpose-scoped, time-bounded, suspendable, or partial consent, and
nothing binds round selection to honour it, so the orchestrator may
keep training between request and claimed erasure, unrecorded. Nor does
running an unlearning algorithm evidence forgetting: approximate
unlearning, the only kind tractable at FL scale, gives no closed-form
guarantee, and its failures are \emph{silent} (an under-erased model
scores like a correctly erased one), while the controller both owes the
erasure and alone reports it. Membership
inference~\cite{shokri2017mia,carlini2022lira,choquettechoo2021labelonly}
detects precisely this residual signal, and the check is not redundant:
on CASAS, gradient ascent, FedEraser, and even exact retraining leave a
signal above threshold (Section~\ref{sec:eval-casas}). \sysname
therefore couples \emph{dynamic consent} (an auditable state gating
every round \emph{before} training) with \emph{verifiable machine
unlearning}, where erasure must pass independent membership-inference
tests before consent advances. Prior threads supply one piece each:
consent ledgers (MedRec~\cite{azaria2016medrec}) cannot gate training;
unlearning methods (SISA~\cite{bourtoule2021sisa},
FedEraser~\cite{liu2021federaser}) leave trigger and verification
off-chain; blockchain-FL~\cite{kim2020blockchained,li2020bflc} logs
updates without consent semantics or forgetting verification.

This work makes four contributions: (1)~a Consent Lifecycle State
Machine (\clsm): six states, nine transitions, four stated safety and
liveness properties, instantiated as a soulbound ERC-721 contract; (2)~a Verifiable Unlearning Protocol (\vup),
method-agnostic over the unlearning algorithm, accepting an attestation
iff three \emph{independent} membership-inference attacks
(shadow~\cite{shokri2017mia}, LiRA~\cite{carlini2022lira},
label-only~\cite{choquettechoo2021labelonly}) all fall below $\tauMIA$
under $t$-of-$n$ ECDSA signatures; (3)~a three-contract Solidity
architecture (ConsentRegistry, UnlearningOracle, FLGatekeeper)
deployed on \emph{both} a gas-free (PureChain, PoA$^2$) and a gas-based
(Polygon Amoy, PoS) chain, the first side-by-side cost comparison
under both economic models; and (4)~a dual-domain evaluation
on a 9-client N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot} autoencoder federation,
cross-validated on a 5-client CASAS BiLSTM classifier, across three
approximate-unlearning paradigms plus exact retraining, measuring
forgetting, on-chain cost, and utility recovery.
