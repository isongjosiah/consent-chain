\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Federated learning (FL) trains a shared model across decentralised
clients without centralising their data~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}, often
the only legally viable option in consumer IoT. Beyond \emph{technical}
privacy, a second, equally binding axis is under-addressed:
\emph{regulatory} privacy. GDPR Article~7
requires consent to be purpose-granular and freely withdrawable, and
Article~17 grants erasure---which, for a trained model, means it must
\emph{forget} a revoked participant; the EU AI Act~\cite{aiact2024}
(Arts.~15/26) and CCPA~\cite{ccpa2018}~\S1798.105 impose analogous,
auditable duties---each demanding audit-grade evidence, not a promise
to comply.

Three research threads each address one corner. Consent-management
ledgers (MedRec~\cite{azaria2016medrec}) record consent but cannot gate
training; machine-unlearning methods (SISA~\cite{bourtoule2021sisa},
FedEraser~\cite{liu2021federaser}) remove a contribution but leave
trigger and verification off-chain; and blockchain-FL
systems~\cite{kim2020blockchained,li2020bflc} log updates without
consent semantics or forgetting verification. No work binds the three.

We frame the gap as a missing \emph{edge}, not a missing vertex:
consent must remain authoritative over which data enters each round,
unlearning (by any method) must be \emph{verifiable} rather than
self-asserted, and verification against membership
inference~\cite{shokri2017mia,carlini2022lira,choquettechoo2021labelonly}
must in turn drive the consent state, closing the loop. Prior work
implements the vertices; \sysname supplies the edges. Enforcing this
off-chain merely moves trust to the controller; a public, gas-free,
PoA$^2$ substrate removes it at \emph{exactly} \$0 per operation, where
a gas-based chain would charge dollars per revocation---prohibitive at
IoT scale. This work makes four contributions: (1)~a \textbf{Consent Lifecycle State
Machine} (\clsm)---six states, nine transitions, four formally-stated
safety and liveness properties, instantiated as a soulbound ERC-721
contract; (2)~a \textbf{Verifiable Unlearning Protocol} (\vup),
method-agnostic over the unlearning algorithm, accepting an attestation
iff three \emph{independent} membership-inference attacks
(shadow~\cite{shokri2017mia}, LiRA~\cite{carlini2022lira},
label-only~\cite{choquettechoo2021labelonly}) all fall below $\tauMIA$
under $t$-of-$n$ ECDSA signatures; (3)~a \textbf{three-contract Solidity
architecture} (ConsentRegistry, UnlearningOracle, FLGatekeeper)
deployed on \emph{both} a gas-free (PureChain, PoA$^2$) and a gas-based
(Polygon Amoy, PoS) chain---the first side-by-side cost comparison
under both economic models; and (4)~a \textbf{dual-domain evaluation}
on a 9-client N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot} autoencoder federation,
cross-validated on a 5-client CASAS BiLSTM classifier, across three
approximate-unlearning paradigms plus exact retraining, measuring
forgetting, on-chain cost, and utility recovery.
