\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
Shadow MIA conflates a client's distributional uniqueness with
membership---naive retrain scored $0.584$/$0.846$ (N-BaIoT/CASAS)
despite never seeing the data---but this is conservative by design: a
compliance gate should prefer false rejection to false acceptance, and
requiring \emph{all three} MIAs below $\tauMIA$ deliberately errs safe.
LiRA resists the confound but is $\alpha$-sensitive, so deployments
co-calibrate $(\alpha,\tauMIA)$ per model class. \sysname accepts
\emph{approximate} unlearning---exact retraining is infeasible at IoT
scale, and GDPR Art.~17's ``reasonable measures'' is met by the on-chain acceptance
predicate with the chain as audit evidence---leaving
formal-erasure guarantees and ECDSA verifier-identity leakage
(BLS-collapsed in the journal version) out of scope. Off-chain
training on revoked data is unpreventable but leaves no UA binding the
deployed model hash to a valid consent state, a publicly detectable
omission.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Regulatory \& deployment.}
\sysname discharges GDPR Arts.~7/17/21, EU AI Act Arts.~15/26, and
CCPA \S~1798.105 through the soulbound NFT, the $\dmax$-bounded
liveness and verifiable-unlearning predicates, and audit completeness
(the four properties above; mapping in supplementary
\texttt{docs/regulatory-alignment.md}).
PureChain's PoA$^2$ is validator-permissioned but publicly readable and
submittable (apt for known-stakeholder IoT consortia); the Amoy
deployment shows the identical contracts on a permissionless PoS chain
at non-zero cost---the economic model is the decision variable, not the
protocol semantics.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Future work:}
(i) a zk-SNARK over the \clsm transition predicate so
\texttt{isEligible} proves eligibility without leaking the subject's
identity or purposes; (ii) PoA$^2$-native verifiers re-running the MIAs
at block production; and (iii) cross-chain consent portability via a
light-client bridge.
