\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent Limitations.
Shadow MIA conflates distributional uniqueness with membership; naive
retrain scored $0.584$/$0.846$ (N-BaIoT/CASAS) despite never seeing the
data. This is conservative by design: a compliance gate should
prefer false rejection to false acceptance, so requiring \emph{all
three} MIAs below $\tauMIA$ deliberately errs safe. LiRA resists the
confound but is $\alpha$-sensitive, so $(\alpha,\tauMIA)$ are
co-calibrated per model class. \sysname accepts \emph{approximate}
unlearning (exact retraining being infeasible at IoT scale, GDPR
Art.~17's ``reasonable measures'' is met by the acceptance predicate
with the chain as audit evidence), leaving formal-erasure guarantees and
ECDSA identity leakage (BLS-collapsed in the journal) out of scope.
Off-chain training on revoked data is unpreventable, but leaves no UA
binding the model to a valid consent state, a detectable omission.

\smallskip\noindent Regulatory \& deployment.
\sysname discharges GDPR Arts.~7/17/21, EU AI Act Arts.~15/26, and CCPA
\S~1798.105 via the soulbound NFT, the $\dmax$-bounded liveness and
verifiability predicates, and audit completeness (mapping in
supplementary \texttt{docs/regulatory-alignment.md}). PoA$^2$ is
validator-permissioned yet publicly readable (apt for
known-stakeholder IoT consortia), while Amoy runs the identical
contracts permissionlessly: the economic model is the decision
variable, not the protocol.
