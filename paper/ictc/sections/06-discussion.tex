\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Our gate is deliberately conservative: shadow MIA conflates
distributional uniqueness with membership (naive retrain, never trained
on the data, scored $0.584$/$0.846$), so requiring \emph{all three} MIAs
below $\tauMIA$ errs toward false rejection, and LiRA, which resists the
confound but is $\alpha$-sensitive, is co-calibrated with $\tauMIA$ per
model class. \sysname targets \emph{approximate} unlearning, meeting
GDPR Art.~17's ``reasonable measures'' by predicate plus on-chain audit
rather than formal erasure; formal-erasure and ECDSA-leakage guarantees
(BLS-collapsed in the journal), and off-chain training on revoked data
(unpreventable, yet a detectable missing UA), stay out of scope. Its
consent, liveness/verifiability, and audit mechanisms discharge GDPR
Arts.~7/17/21, EU AI Act Arts.~15/26, and CCPA \S~1798.105, and run
unchanged on a permissioned-yet-public PoA$^2$ chain or permissionless
Amoy, so the economic model, not the protocol, is the deployment
variable.

\sysname is, to our knowledge, the first protocol to bind dynamic
consent, machine unlearning, and on-chain verification in one
chain-enforced pipeline, closing the consent--unlearning--verification
triangle prior work addressed only at the vertices. Across 9-client
N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot} and 5-client CASAS, approximate
unlearning drives all three MIAs below $\tauMIA\!=\!0.55$ at
$\le\!3\%$ utility loss, with per-subject lifecycle cost $\$0$ on
PureChain versus $\$0.00120$ on Amoy. Beyond the BLS aggregation and
formal-erasure guarantees noted above, future work targets
zero-knowledge eligibility proofs, PoA$^2$-native verifiers, and
cross-chain consent portability.
