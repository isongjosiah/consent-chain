\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \sysname, the first protocol we are aware of to bind
dynamic consent, machine unlearning, and on-chain verification into a
single chain-enforced pipeline, closing the
consent--unlearning--verification triangle that prior work addressed
only at the vertices. It decomposes into a formally-stated Consent
Lifecycle State Machine implemented as soulbound ERC-721 tokens, a
method-agnostic Verifiable Unlearning Protocol gated on three
independent MIA scores and a $t$-of-$n$ ECDSA committee signature, and
a three-contract Solidity architecture that runs unchanged on gas-free
and gas-based EVM substrates. Evaluation on a 9-client
N-BaIoT~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot} federated autoencoder, cross-validated
on a 5-client CASAS BiLSTM activity classifier, confirms four headline
numbers: per-data-subject on-chain lifecycle cost of $\$0$ on PureChain
versus $\$0.00120$ on Polygon Amoy; \texttt{validateRound} scaling
linearly to $N\!=\!500$ at $\$0$ on PureChain versus a projected
$\$0.0176$ on Amoy; MIA scores driven below the on-chain
$\tauMIA\!=\!0.55$ threshold by approximate unlearning (all three
methods on N-BaIoT, SISA-Fed on CASAS); and an absolute utility delta
of $\le\!3\%$ on two of three methods---in fact a $6.1\%$ utility
\emph{gain} when the revoked client was an outlier. Future work targets
BLS threshold-signature aggregation, zero-knowledge consent eligibility
proofs, PoA$^2$-native verifier-committee integration, and cross-chain
consent portability.
