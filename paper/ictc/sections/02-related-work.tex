\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\smallskip
MedRec~\cite{azaria2016medrec} records patient consent immutably
on-chain (the durability and owner-exclusive control we inherit), but
treats the record as the protocol \emph{endpoint}: no training is gated
by it, and nothing shows a withdrawal produced a model update.
\emph{Gap}: consent is recorded but does not influence training.

\smallskip
SISA~\cite{bourtoule2021sisa} pre-shards data so removal retrains only
the affected shard; FedEraser~\cite{liu2021federaser} replays
aggregation history with calibration; gradient-ascent methods take a few
negative-rate steps on the data to remove. None of these, nor the
broader federated-unlearning literature~\cite{romandini2024fusurvey},
specifies how unlearning is triggered, by whom, or how it binds back to
consent. \emph{Gap}: unlearning is implementable but not bound
end-to-end to the consent that should trigger it.

\smallskip
BlockFL~\cite{kim2020blockchained} logs device-side updates on-chain and
BFLC~\cite{li2020bflc} replaces the aggregator with committee consensus,
but neither encodes consent. Closest to us, Zuo et
al.~\cite{zuo2024blockchain} certify IoT federated unlearning on-chain;
yet their ledger records that an unlearning routine \emph{ran}, not that
the model \emph{forgot}, and no consent lifecycle gates training.
Membership inference~\cite{shokri2017mia,carlini2022lira,choquettechoo2021labelonly}
is the canonical forgetting test, though a single attack can overstate
it~\cite{hayes2024inexact}, motivating our three-attack conjunction.
\emph{Gap}: updates are auditable, but consent semantics and
\emph{measured} forgetting verification are absent.

\smallskip
Across the four dimensions (consent representation, unlearning
trigger/method, verification, on-chain footprint), each prior system
covers at most one. \sysname addresses all four in one chain-enforced
protocol at gas-free cost: MedRec's registry (vertex~A) under \clsm
properties, any unlearning paradigm as a black box (vertex~B), and
three-MIA verification (vertex~C) gating the consent transition: the
missing edge. Chain choice matters at IoT scale; we quantify this
(Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}) against PureChain~\cite{kim2025purechain},
a public, gas-free, EVM-compatible PoA$^2$ chain, a substrate rather
than a contribution, on which the protocol is identical to a gas-based
chain while the economics differ fundamentally.
