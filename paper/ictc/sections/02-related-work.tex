\section{related work}
\label{sec:related}

\vspace{-0.3cm}
Three threads each solve part of the problem but none their conjunction.
MedRec~\cite{azaria2016medrec} records consent
immutably yet treats the record as an endpoint: no training is gated by
it. Federated-unlearning methods, SISA~\cite{bourtoule2021sisa} (shard
retraining), FedEraser~\cite{liu2021federaser} (history replay), and
gradient ascent, remove a contribution but, like the broader
survey~\cite{romandini2024fusurvey}, never specify how unlearning is
triggered or bound back to consent. Blockchain-FL brings computation
on-chain without consent: BlockFL~\cite{kim2020blockchained} and
BFLC~\cite{li2020bflc} log updates yet let no participant withdraw, and
the closest work, Zuo et al.~\cite{zuo2024blockchain}, certifies that an
unlearning routine \emph{ran}, not that the model \emph{forgot}, the very
gap membership
inference~\cite{shokri2017mia,carlini2022lira,choquettechoo2021labelonly}
measures (a single attack can overstate it~\cite{hayes2024inexact},
motivating our three-attack conjunction).

Each prior system thus covers at most one dimension; \sysname supplies
the missing edges between them: consent state gating rounds under \clsm properties, any
unlearning paradigm as a black box, and three-MIA verification driving the consent
transition. Chain choice is an economic, not a protocol, decision: identical contracts run on
PureChain~\cite{ajakwe2025ebctc}, a public gas-free PoA$^2$ chain, and gas-based Amoy, a difference we quantify in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}. Table~\ref{tab:related} summarises this positioning.
