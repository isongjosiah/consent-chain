\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Three threads each approach part of the problem. Consent on-chain comes
first: MedRec~\cite{azaria2016medrec} records patient consent immutably
(the durability and owner-exclusive control we inherit), but treats the
record as the protocol \emph{endpoint}: no training is gated by it, and
nothing shows a withdrawal produced a model update. The consent record
is authoritative yet inert.

That inertness matters because the unlearning a withdrawal should
trigger is itself unanchored. SISA~\cite{bourtoule2021sisa} pre-shards
data so removal retrains only the affected shard;
FedEraser~\cite{liu2021federaser} replays aggregation history with
calibration; gradient-ascent methods take a few negative-rate steps.
None of these, nor the broader survey~\cite{romandini2024fusurvey},
specifies how unlearning is triggered, by whom, or how it binds back to
consent: the mechanism exists but hangs free of the consent that should
invoke it.

Blockchain-FL brings computation on-chain but still not consent:
BlockFL~\cite{kim2020blockchained} logs device updates and
BFLC~\cite{li2020bflc} swaps the aggregator for committee consensus, yet
a joined participant cannot withdraw without trusting the orchestrator.
Closest to us, Zuo et al.~\cite{zuo2024blockchain} certify IoT federated
unlearning on-chain, but their ledger records that an unlearning routine
\emph{ran}, not that the model \emph{forgot}. Whether it forgot is what
membership inference~\cite{shokri2017mia,carlini2022lira,choquettechoo2021labelonly}
measures, though a single attack can overstate
it~\cite{hayes2024inexact}, motivating our three-attack conjunction; yet
no prior system uses these scores as an on-chain acceptance criterion.

Each thread thus supplies one vertex, consent representation, an
unlearning method, a forgetting test, but none of the edges between
them. \sysname binds all three in one chain-enforced protocol at
gas-free cost: MedRec's registry (vertex~A) under \clsm properties, any
unlearning paradigm as a black box (vertex~B), and three-MIA
verification (vertex~C) gating the consent transition. Chain choice
matters at IoT scale; we quantify this
(Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}) against PureChain~\cite{kim2025purechain},
a public, gas-free, EVM-compatible PoA$^2$ chain, a substrate rather
than a contribution, on which the protocol is identical to a gas-based
chain while the economics differ fundamentally.
