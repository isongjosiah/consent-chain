\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate \sysname along four empirical axes: (i) unlearning
effectiveness under three approximate methods plus an
exact-retraining baseline (E6), (ii) on-chain cost on PureChain
versus Polygon Amoy (E7), (iii) scalability of round validation up
to $N\!=\!500$ participants (E8), and (iv) utility recovery through
the revocation event. Two short on-chain demos additionally validate
Properties~\ref{prop:liveness} and~\ref{prop:verifiability}, and
\S~\ref{sec:eval-casas} cross-validates the same pipeline on CASAS.
The complete reproducibility recipe lives in supplementary
\texttt{docs/eval-protocol.md}.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

The primary federation is a 9-client N-BaIoT autoencoder anomaly
detector~\cite{meidan2018nbaiot}, one client per IoT device (doorbells,
thermostats, baby monitors, security cameras, webcams). Each client
holds 5{,}000 benign-traffic rows over 115 network features; the
global model is a symmetric MLP autoencoder
($115\!\to\!75\!\to\!50\!\to\!25\!\to\!10$, ReLU, dropout 0.1) trained
with FedAvg over 30 rounds (Flower framework~\cite{beutel2020flower}),
batch size 256, learning rate $10^{-3}$, random seed 42. At round 30 we simulate a GDPR Art.~17 erasure event
against client 5 (\textit{Provision\_PT\_838\_Security\_Camera}) and
trigger one of three approximate unlearning methods (SISA-Fed,
gradient ascent, FedEraser) or, as the exact reference, full
retraining of an 8-client federation from scratch. All experiments
record an environment fingerprint (commit hash, library versions,
hardware, seed) in an \texttt{env.json} alongside the metric JSON.

On-chain experiments run against the live contracts deployed on both
PureChain (chainId \texttt{900520900520}, gas-free, PoA$^2$) and
Polygon Amoy (chainId \texttt{80002}, gas-based, PoS). The verifier
set $\mathit{VC}$ is five ECDSA keys with threshold $t = 3$;
$\dmax = 1000$ blocks unless otherwise noted.

\subsection{Unlearning effectiveness}
\label{sec:eval-unlearning}

Table~\ref{tab:t1} reports each method's three independent MIA
scores against the revoked client's member set. The on-chain
\texttt{submitAttestation} predicate (Eq.~\ref{eq:accept}) accepts a
UA iff all three scores sit below $\tauMIA\!=\!0.55$.

\centering
\caption{Table 1 --- Unlearning effectiveness (E6). MIA scores

\label{tab:t1}
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{../results/tables/table1_unlearning.tex}}

Three observations stand out. All three approximate methods produce
UAs the chain accepts (shadow $0.438$/$0.254$/$0.523$ for gradient
ascent/SISA-Fed/FedEraser; LiRA and label-only well below
$\tauMIA$). The naive-retrain baseline --- a model that
\emph{never saw} client 5's data --- yields shadow $0.584 >
\tauMIA$ and is \emph{rejected}: shadow picks up client 5's
distinctive traffic distribution, not membership, a known
confound~\cite{shokri2017mia} that LiRA ($0.045$) and label-only
($0.014$) do not exhibit on the same model. The protocol is thus
faithful to MIA evidence, not the controller's claim
(\S~\ref{sec:discussion}). Gradient ascent's shadow ($0.438$) even
sits \emph{below} naive retrain's: anti-fitting moves the model
further from client 5's distribution than never seeing it. The
methods trace a forgetting--utility frontier --- SISA-Fed
strong-forgetting, gradient ascent / FedEraser preserve-utility ---
made explicit by the recovery curves (\S~\ref{sec:eval-utility}).
All three UAs executed end-to-end on PureChain
(\texttt{transitionToUnlearned} in the same tx as
\texttt{submitAttestation}, final
$\mathit{CR}.\mathtt{stateOf}=\textsc{Unlearned}$; representative
gradient-ascent tx \texttt{0x5beb\ldots 416a}, full records in
supplementary \texttt{onchain\_attestation.json}).

\subsection{Scalability of round validation}
\label{sec:eval-scalability}

Figure~\ref{fig:f1} reports gas usage and equivalent USD cost for
$\texttt{validateRound}(N)$ as $N$ scales from 5 to 500 participants.
On PureChain the full sweep ran live; on Amoy only $N \in \{5, 10\}$
were measured directly (wallet-budget constraint) and the remaining
points are extrapolated via least-squares linear fit
$\mathrm{gas}(N) \approx 40{,}002 + 12{,}202 N$, which the
PureChain measurements (slope $7{,}616$) confirm as exactly linear
($R^2 \!\approx\! 1$).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth]{figures/figure1_scalability.pdf}
\caption{Figure 1 --- Scalability of $\texttt{validateRound}(N)$
(E8). Solid lines: live gas measurements. Dashed lines: per-round
USD cost. PureChain cost line is at zero across all $N$; Amoy
projects to $\$0.0176$ at $N\!=\!500$.}
\label{fig:f1}
\end{figure}

PureChain's per-participant slope is $\sim\!38\%$ lower than Amoy's
--- the EIP-2929 cold-SLOAD pricing ($2{,}100$ gas) dominates the
linear term on Amoy. The cost story is dominated by gas \emph{price}:
on Amoy a single $N\!=\!500$ round-validation costs $\$0.0176$; on
PureChain the same operation costs $\$0$. Combined with a typical
100-FL-rounds-per-day cadence, Amoy accumulates $\$1.76$/day per
deployment; 10 concurrent deployments would cost $\sim\!\$17.57$/day
on Amoy and $\$0$ on PureChain. Gas-free infrastructure is not an
optimisation; it is a different cost class.

\subsection{Utility recovery after revocation}
\label{sec:eval-utility}

Figure~\ref{fig:f2} plots the size-weighted central reconstruction
error for five trajectories diverging at round 30, the revocation
point. The baseline 9-client federation continues to round 40 with
all clients; the other four trajectories drop client 5 and resume
FL for ten further rounds, each starting from a different round-30
checkpoint.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth]{figures/figure2_utility_recovery.pdf}
\caption{Figure 2 --- Utility recovery (E6). Centralized test
reconstruction error per FL round. The four post-revocation
trajectories diverge at round 30; SISA-Fed pays a real utility cost,
gradient ascent and FedEraser converge to within $0.5\%$ of plain
post-revoke by round 40.}
\label{fig:f2}
\end{figure}

The post-revoke trajectory ends at $0.4720$, $6.1\%$
\emph{better} than the 9-client baseline ($0.5025$) --- client 5
was an outlier whose removal lets the federation fit the remaining
devices more tightly. Gradient ascent ($0.4726$) and FedEraser
($0.4698$) match this with no utility cost. SISA-Fed pays a real
cost ($0.7009$ at round 31, recovering to $0.6081$ by round 40 ---
$+29\%$), consistent with its strongest-forgetting position and
lowest shadow MIA in Table~\ref{tab:t1}.

\subsection{On-chain property demos}
\label{sec:eval-props}

Two short PureChain runs validate the formal properties.
\textbf{Liveness} (Property~\ref{prop:liveness}): with
$\dmax\!=\!2$, \texttt{reportTimeout} reverts
(\texttt{NotYetTimedOut}) pre-deadline and emits
\texttt{ComplianceViolation} ($24{,}800$ gas) once past it.
\textbf{Verifiability} (Property~\ref{prop:verifiability}): a
$t$-of-5 sweep over $t\!\in\!\{2,3,4,5\}$ confirms both branches
of Eq.~\ref{eq:verifiability} --- $t$ signatures accept, $t\!-\!1$
revert with \texttt{InsufficientSignatures} (marginal
$\sim\!7{,}800$ gas/sig; default $t\!=\!3$ totals $102{,}941$ gas).

\subsection{Cross-domain validation (CASAS)}
\label{sec:eval-casas}

We replayed \S\ref{sec:eval-unlearning}--\ref{sec:eval-utility} on
the CASAS smart-home benchmark (5-client BiLSTM, per-apartment
sensor streams, \texttt{twor\_2010} revoked at round 60). The
distribution-shift confound is sharper here: shadow rejects
pre-unlearn, naive retrain, gradient ascent, and FedEraser (all
$\ge\!0.84$), so SISA-Fed ($0.499/0.035/0.000$ shadow/LiRA/label)
is the \emph{only} method passing the $\tauMIA\!=\!0.55$ gate ---
the strongest-forgetting Pareto endpoint, consistent with N-BaIoT.
A 15\%-ratio backdoor canary triggers $100\%$ pre-unlearning and
drops to $0\%$ under SISA-Fed and FedEraser (gradient ascent
preserves it at 10 steps). End-to-end SISA-Fed attestation on
PureChain (\texttt{submitAttestation} \texttt{0x9643\ldots 733b})
transitioned token 527 to \textsc{Unlearned}.

Table~\ref{tab:t3} positions \sysname against the three closest
prior systems along four dimensions. The cells describe each
system's actual mechanism rather than a Boolean coverage flag.
MedRec established the on-chain consent pattern we build on
(rows 1, 4) but treats the consent contract as the protocol
endpoint with no downstream training gate (rows 2--3). FedEraser
contributes a federated unlearning algorithm (row 2) that \sysname
accepts as one of its three pluggable methods; the trigger,
verification, and audit dimensions are out of scope in the original
work. BlockFL addresses aggregator trust through on-chain
model-update logs (row 4) but encodes no consent semantics.
\sysname is the first design we are aware of that addresses all
four dimensions in a single chain-enforced protocol, and the first
to do so at gas-free per-operation cost on a public EVM-compatible
substrate.
