\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Limitations}

\noindent\textbf{MIA selection sensitivity.}
The shadow attack picks up client 5's distributional uniqueness as
much as membership signal --- the naive-retrain baseline scored
$0.584$ in Table~\ref{tab:t1} despite never having seen the data.
Stronger overfitting regimes would tighten the gap; deployments
should recalibrate $\tauMIA$ on the specific
MIA-classifier output distribution. LiRA returned exactly $0.500$
on every model in our setup because the 30-round autoencoder simply
does not memorise individual rows hard enough for the
likelihood-ratio test to detect~\cite{carlini2022lira}; the on-chain
gate is robust to this because it requires \emph{all three} MIAs
below $\tauMIA$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Approximate-versus-exact unlearning.}
\sysname accepts approximate methods (gradient ascent, FedEraser,
SISA-Fed) because exact retraining is economically infeasible at
IoT scale. GDPR Art.~17 requires ``reasonable measures'' --- we
argue Eq.~\ref{eq:verifiability} implements this standard, with the
chain providing the audit evidence. Formal information-theoretic
erasure guarantees and ECDSA verifier-identity leakage (collapsed by
BLS aggregation in the journal version) are out of scope for the
conference prototype.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Off-chain misuse.} A data controller could
train on revoked data off-chain and never submit through
\texttt{validateRound}. The protocol does not prevent this, but
any audited deployment would have no UA tying the deployed model
hash to a valid consent state --- the deviation is publicly
detectable through the chain's negative evidence.

\subsection{Regulatory alignment}

\sysname binds to three regulatory regimes (full mapping in
supplementary \texttt{docs/regulatory-alignment.md}). GDPR
Article~7~\cite{gdpr2016} is discharged by the soulbound NFT with
$\mathit{dsOwner}$-restricted transitions and per-purpose
$\mathcal{P}_i$ binding; Article~17 by the
$\dmax$-bounded liveness of Property~\ref{prop:liveness} and the
verifiable-unlearning predicate of
Property~\ref{prop:verifiability}; Article~21 by the
$\texttt{suspend/resume}$ edges of the \clsm. EU AI
Act~\cite{aiact2024} Articles~15 and~26 are addressed by the
auditability of Property~\ref{prop:audit} and the
permissionless monitoring path through $\texttt{reportTimeout}$.
CCPA~\cite{ccpa2018} \S~1798.105 maps directly to the same
mechanisms as GDPR Article~17.

\subsection{Permissioned versus permissionless deployments}

PureChain's PoA$^2$ consensus is permissioned at the validator
layer but public at the read and transaction-submission layers ---
an asymmetry appropriate for IoT consortia with known stakeholders.
The Polygon Amoy deployment demonstrates that the same contracts run
unchanged on a permissionless PoS chain at non-zero per-operation
cost; deployments that cannot accept a permissioned validator set
can choose Amoy (or mainnet Polygon, or any EVM-compatible chain),
paying for that choice through gas. The economic model is the
decision variable; the protocol semantics are identical.

\subsection{Future work}

Three extensions are queued for the journal version. (i)
\emph{Zero-knowledge consent proofs}: the eligibility check
$\texttt{isEligible}(i, \rho_r, \cdot)$ currently leaks the
data subject's address and purpose set to anyone observing the
chain; a zk-SNARK over the \clsm transition predicate would
prove eligibility without revealing identity. (ii)
\emph{PoA$^2$-native verifier integration}: rather than a
separate $\mathit{VC}$, the consensus validators themselves
re-run the MIAs during block production; UA acceptance becomes a
consensus-time predicate, eliminating the off-chain coordination
loop. (iii) \emph{Cross-chain consent portability}: a consent
granted on PureChain becomes redeemable on Polygon or other L2s
via a light-client bridge, so a data subject does not need to
re-grant per chain when participating in multiple federations.
