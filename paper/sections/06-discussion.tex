\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Limitations}

\noindent\textbf{MIA selection and calibration.}
The shadow attack picks up the revoked client's distributional
uniqueness as much as membership signal --- the naive-retrain
baseline scored $0.584$ (N-BaIoT) and $0.846$ (CASAS) in
Table~\ref{tab:t1} despite never seeing the data. LiRA's per-row
likelihood-ratio test resists this confound (naive-retrain
$\le\!0.05$ on both at $\alpha\!=\!0.05$) but is itself sensitive
to the FPR parameter $\alpha$: at $\alpha\!=\!0.05$ the gate admits
the pre-unlearn baseline, at $\alpha\!=\!0.5$ it correctly rejects
it. The on-chain gate is robust to either choice because it
requires \emph{all three} MIAs below $\tauMIA$; deployments should
nonetheless co-calibrate $(\alpha,\tauMIA)$ against the target's
leakage profile per model class.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Approximate unlearning and off-chain
misuse.} \sysname accepts approximate methods because exact
retraining is infeasible at IoT scale; GDPR Art.~17's ``reasonable
measures'' standard is met by Eq.~\ref{eq:verifiability} with the
chain as audit evidence. Formal erasure guarantees and ECDSA
verifier-identity leakage (BLS-collapsed in the journal version)
are out of conference scope. A controller could also train on
revoked data entirely off-chain --- the protocol cannot prevent
this, but an audited deployment then has no UA binding the
deployed model hash to a valid consent state, so the deviation is
publicly detectable as on-chain negative evidence.

\subsection{Regulatory alignment}

\sysname binds three regimes (full mapping in supplementary
\texttt{docs/regulatory-alignment.md}). GDPR~\cite{gdpr2016}
Art.~7 is met by the soulbound NFT's
$\mathit{dsOwner}$-restricted, per-purpose transitions; Art.~17 by
the $\dmax$-bounded liveness (Property~\ref{prop:liveness}) and
verifiable-unlearning predicate (Property~\ref{prop:verifiability});
Art.~21 by the $\texttt{suspend}/\texttt{resume}$ edges. EU AI
Act~\cite{aiact2024} Arts.~15/26 are met by the audit completeness
(Property~\ref{prop:audit}) and the permissionless
$\texttt{reportTimeout}$ path; CCPA~\cite{ccpa2018} \S~1798.105
maps onto the same mechanisms as GDPR Art.~17.

\subsection{Permissioned versus permissionless deployments}

PureChain's PoA$^2$ is permissioned at the validator layer but
public for reads and transaction submission --- appropriate for
IoT consortia with known stakeholders. The Polygon Amoy
deployment shows the identical contracts running on a
permissionless PoS chain at non-zero per-operation cost: the
economic model is the decision variable, the protocol semantics
are not.

\subsection{Future work}

Three extensions are queued for the journal version.
(i)~\emph{Zero-knowledge consent proofs}: a zk-SNARK over the
\clsm transition predicate would let
$\texttt{isEligible}(i,\rho_r,\cdot)$ prove eligibility without
leaking the data subject's address or purpose set.
(ii)~\emph{PoA$^2$-native verifiers}: consensus validators re-run
the MIAs during block production, making UA acceptance a
consensus-time predicate and eliminating the off-chain
coordination loop. (iii)~\emph{Cross-chain consent portability}:
a consent granted on PureChain becomes redeemable on other L2s
via a light-client bridge, removing per-chain re-granting for
federations that span multiple EVM chains.
